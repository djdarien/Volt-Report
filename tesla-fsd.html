Full Self-Driving

FSD (Supervised) is now two products wearing one name. Version 14.3 brings the full new model to HW4/AI4 cars. Hardware 3 cars get FSD v14.3 Lite — a distilled build that is far better than what came before, and very likely the end of the line.

HW4 · v14.3 HW3 · v14.3 Lite

Hardware 4 / AI4

FSD v14.3 — the full model

v14.3 is the current FSD (Supervised) branch for cars on the newest AI4 computer. The latest build is v14.3.13 (November 4, 2026, on software 2026.38.5.12). Per Tesla's release notes, the 14.3 generation brings:

  • One unified model across Summon, FSD, and Robotaxi driving (HW4 only)
  • A rewritten AI compiler — Tesla claims roughly 20% faster reactions
  • A new vision encoder feeding the driving model
  • Automatic Collision Evasion (AI4 only) — the car can engage FSD on its own if a collision looks imminent

This is the branch that gets the frontier features first, and the one Tesla's robotaxi work is built on.

Hardware 3 / AI3

FSD v14.3 Lite — the distilled build

"FSD v14.3 Lite" is Tesla's official name for the HW3 version: the v14 model distilled down to run on the older computer. The first v14 Lite release landed June 29, 2026; Intel-based HW3 Model S and X cars received 14.3 Lite on October 14, 2026. Lite includes:

  • Better merges and highway fork decisions
  • Sharper pedestrian and traffic-light responses
  • Fewer false slowdowns, smoother steering
  • Parking, unparking, and reversing
  • Arrival Options and Speed Profiles — including the new, relaxed SLOTH profile (a few features are missing specifically on Intel-based Model S/X)
  • Start FSD from Park

Lite excludes the headline HW4 items: no unified Summon/FSD/Robotaxi model, no Automatic Collision Evasion, and no unsupervised capability. Tesla has been clear that HW3 will not get unsupervised FSD or a v15 branch — v14 Lite is, in all likelihood, the last major FSD generation for these cars.

What this means for HW3 owners like us. This site is run from a 2022 Model 3 — an HW3 car. The honest summary: 14.3 Lite is genuinely excellent day to day (see the hands-on review), and it is also a ceiling. If unsupervised driving is the feature you are waiting for, Tesla's own messaging says it will take newer hardware. If you bought FSD to make today's drives calmer, Lite delivers that — and then some.

Pricing (US, 2026)

  • Autopilot: standard
  • Enhanced Autopilot: discontinued as a new purchase
  • FSD (Supervised): $99/mo or $999/yr
  • EAP owners: $49/mo
  • One-time FSD buy ended February 14, 2026 (last price $8,000). Existing VIN licenses stay on those cars.

Safety & oversight

On every car, on every branch, FSD remains SAE Level 2: a supervised driver-assistance system, not autonomy. Keep your attention on the road, hands ready, and take over in rain, construction, or anything unclear.

For context on the regulatory side: NHTSA's probe into FSD performance in reduced visibility was upgraded to an Engineering Analysis (EA26002, March 18, 2026) covering roughly 3.2 million vehicles. An engineering analysis is an investigation stage — it is not a defect finding — and we report it that way.
