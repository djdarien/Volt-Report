The Reveal

A concept car grows up — mostly

The second-generation Roadster has been "coming next year" since November 2017, when the original prototype appeared in the back of the Tesla Semi. On October 15, 2026, at SpaceX's McGregor, Texas facility, Tesla presented what it calls a production-intent car: the design is locked, the trims are defined, and reservations — which quietly reopened in September 2026 — are taking real deposits again.

Elon Musk put production at 2028. Read that the way you should read every Roadster date: as a target, not a promise. Tesla has not guaranteed a delivery date, and no final pricing has been published. What has changed is the shape of the offer — the one Roadster of 2017 is now three very different machines.

The Lineup

Three trims, three levels of seriousness

Performance

<1.6s
0–60 mph · Tesla claim

The "regular" Roadster, if a two-seat missile counts as regular. The headline tech is aerodynamic: a suction-fan ground-effect system that pulls the car toward the road, plus steer-by-wire.

  • 2 seats
  • Suction-fan ground effect
  • Steer-by-wire
  • Reservation deposit: $50,000

Thrust

<0.9s
0–60 mph · Tesla claim

The SpaceX package grows up. Cold-gas thrusters at the rear shove the car off the line hard enough to duck under the one-second barrier — in a limited release for buyers who want the party trick.

  • Rear SpaceX cold-gas thrusters
  • Limited release
  • Reservation deposit: $250,000

Rocket

<0.59s
0–60 mph · Tesla claim

A single-seat experiment with thrusters all around, demoed on stage doing a brief hop. Tesla's own page is blunt about it: the Rocket is "not approved for public road use." Invite-only, and best understood as a halo stunt.

  • 1 seat
  • Thrusters all around
  • Brief hop/flight demoed at the reveal
  • Invite-only

On stage, Tesla timed a 0.81-second 0–60 run in front of the crowd — a demonstration the company staged and measured itself. It is spectacular theater. It is not an independent test, and no outside instrument has verified any of the three trims' numbers yet.

The honest read: the sprint claims got wilder while the range claim got realistic. A 620-mile sports car was always the least believable line of the 2017 show; 300+ miles in a car built to accelerate like this is a trade most buyers will take. The top-speed step back — 250+ down to "over 230" — is the same story: numbers moving from poster copy toward engineering.

Reservations

Want one? Bring a deposit and some patience

  • Performance: $50,000 deposit
  • Thrust: $250,000 deposit, limited release
  • Rocket: invite-only
  • Production: targeted to begin in 2028, per Musk at the reveal
  • Final pricing: not announced for any trim

Reservations reopened in September 2026 after years in limbo. If the nine-year wait taught reservation holders anything, it is that a deposit buys a place in line — not a date. Official details and the reservation flow live on Tesla's Roadster page.

Claims vs. verified — our house rule. Every performance figure on this page is a Tesla claim from the October 2026 reveal or Tesla's own product page, labeled as such. None has been independently tested. The on-stage 0.81s run was timed by Tesla. The Rocket's hop was a demonstration, and Tesla states the Rocket trim is not approved for public road use. We will update this page when — and only when — outside instruments or customer deliveries put real numbers on the car.
